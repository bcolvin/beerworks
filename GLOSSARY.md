# Beerworks

Domain terminology for beer style reference guidelines, hop varieties, and sensory evaluation in the Beerworks suite.

## Language

### Core Ingredient Architecture

**Ingredient**:
A canonical botanical, grain, or fermentable ingredient (Hops, Malts, Honey, Pomes, or Yeast) providing flavor, aroma, color, fermentable sugars, or fermentation character, sharing common catalog attributes (identity, botanical provenance, geographic origin, descriptors, sensory profiles, and beverage style pairings).
_Avoid_: Raw material, brewing component, supply item

### Hops & Ingredients

**Hop Variety**:
A distinct cultivated variety of the hop plant (*Humulus lupulus*) defined by specific agronomic, chemical, and sensory characteristics.
_Avoid_: Hop strain, hop type

**Lineage**:
The botanical parentage and breeding progeny of a hop variety, identifying parent cultivars and descendant varieties.
_Avoid_: Pedigree, family tree

**Purpose**:
The primary brewing application of a hop variety, classified as Bittering, Aroma, or Dual-Purpose based on alpha acid concentration and essential oil composition.
_Avoid_: Hop role, usage category

**Sensory Category**:
A standardized high-level organoleptic grouping (such as Citrus, Tropical Fruit, Floral, Herbal, Spicy, Pine/Resinous, Earthy/Woody) used to classify hop aroma profiles.
_Avoid_: Flavor group, aroma family

**Sensory Descriptor**:
A specific descriptive term (e.g., grapefruit, passionfruit, cedar, black pepper) identifying a nuanced aroma or flavor note within a sensory category.
_Avoid_: Tasting note, flavor tag

**Sensory Radar Chart**:
A multi-axis radial polygon chart plotting quantitative sensory intensity (0–5) across primary hop aroma dimensions (Citrus, Tropical, Floral, Herbal, Spicy, Pine, Earthy, Grassy).
_Avoid_: Spider graph, aroma wheel

**Alpha Acid**:
The primary resinous compound class (humulone group) in hops that isomerizes during boiling to impart bitterness to beer.
_Avoid_: Bittering rating, AA% (in formal descriptions)

**Beta Acid**:
The secondary resinous compound class (lupulone group) in hops that contributes subtle bitterness through oxidative aging rather than thermal isomerization.
_Avoid_: Preservative rating, BA% (in formal descriptions)

**Hop Substitute**:
A hop cultivar possessing sufficiently similar alpha acid potential, essential oil ratios, and sensory descriptors to serve as an interchangeable alternative in recipe formulation.
_Avoid_: Replacement hop, alternative variety

**Common Hops**:
The primary hop varieties traditionally, historically, or commercially associated with a specific beer style in brewing literature and competition guidelines.
_Avoid_: Featured hops, style hops, associated hops

### Malts & Fermentables

**Malt Variety**:
A distinct type of malted or unmalted brewing grain or fermentable characterized by specific malting/kilning processes, color rating, diastatic power, and organoleptic properties.
_Avoid_: Malt type, grain variety

**Malt Category**:
A standardized classification of brewing grains based on production method and recipe function: Base Malt, Kilned & Toasted, Caramel & Crystal, Roasted & Dark, or Adjunct & Raw Grain.
_Avoid_: Grain class, malt family

**Diastatic Power**:
The enzymatic capacity of malted grain to convert starches into fermentable sugars during mashing, measured in degrees Lintner or qualitative conversion levels.
_Avoid_: Enzyme level, conversion strength

**Extract Potential**:
The laboratory yield of soluble extract obtained from grain under standardized fine-grind conditions (Dry Basis Fine Grind %), indicating gravity contribution per pound.
_Avoid_: Sugar yield, PPG potential

**Typical Usage**:
The primary brewing application, recipe role, and maximum grist percentage recommended for a malt (e.g., base grist, foam stability, color adjustment, roasted notes).
_Avoid_: Usage notes, recipe tips

**Functional Usage Tag**:
A standardized classification of a malt's specific physical or recipe contribution in brewing (e.g., Base Grist, Head Retention, Body & Mouthfeel, Color Adjustment, Caramel Sweetness, Toasty Aroma, Roast & Coffee, Haze & Cloudiness).
_Avoid_: Brewing role, grain function

**Malt Sensory Radar Chart**:
An 8-axis radial polygon chart plotting quantitative sensory intensity (0–5) across primary malt flavor dimensions (Malty/Sweet, Bready/Biscuit, Toasty, Caramel/Toffee, Nutty, Chocolate, Roast/Coffee, Grainy/Husky).
_Avoid_: Malt spider graph, grain wheel

**Malt Substitute**:
A malt product or combination of grains providing equivalent color, extract, and flavor characteristics that can be swapped into a grist formulation without altering style character.
_Avoid_: Alternative malt, replacement grain

**Commercial Malts**:
Representative commercial brand offerings and notable maltster products (e.g., Weyermann Carafa, Briess Carapils, Dingemans Special B) exemplifying a canonical malt variety.
_Avoid_: Malt brands, proprietary grains

### Beer Styles & Evaluation

**Beer Style**:
A distinct recognized category of beer specified by sensory attributes, cultural history, and technical brewing parameters in official guidelines (BJCP or Brewers Association).
_Avoid_: Beer type, style profile

**Base Style Group**:
A top-level grouping of related beer styles sharing common lineage or geographical origin (e.g., IPA, Standard American Beer, Pale Malty European Lager).
_Avoid_: Supercategory, broad style

**Side-by-Side Comparison**:
A concurrent comparative evaluation of two or three beer styles, hop varieties, or malt profiles aligning technical parameters, sensory radars, and flavor descriptors in a multi-column matrix.
_Avoid_: Diff view, duel mode

### Honey & Varietals

**Honey Variety**:
A distinct varietal honey characterized by botanical source, floral classification, geographic origin, Pfund color rating, and organoleptic and fermentation properties.
_Avoid_: Honey type, honey flavor, nectar strain

**Floral Classification**:
The classification of honey by nectar source: Monofloral (single dominant botanical blossom), Polyfloral (wildflower nectar from diverse flora), or Honeydew (secretions collected from plant-sucking insects rather than blossom nectar).
_Avoid_: Honey source, floral category, nectar type

**Pfund Scale**:
A standardized optical scale measuring honey color intensity in millimeters (1–140 mm), corresponding to official USDA color grades from Water White to Dark Amber.
_Avoid_: Honey darkness, color rating, tint scale

**USDA Honey Color Classification**:
The standardized seven-tier visual grading system for honey based on Pfund scale optical density: Water White (0–8 mm), Extra White (9–17 mm), White (18–34 mm), Extra Light Amber (35–50 mm), Light Amber (51–85 mm), Amber (86–114 mm), and Dark Amber (> 114 mm).
_Avoid_: Color grade, honey shade, visual tier

**Crystallization Tendency**:
The relative rate and propensity of a honey variety to granulate at room temperature based on its Fructose-to-Glucose (F/G) ratio and glucose-to-water ratio, categorized as Slow, Moderate, or Rapid.
_Avoid_: Granulation rate, sugar hardening, solidifying speed

**Honey Sensory Radar Chart**:
An 8-axis radial polygon chart plotting quantitative sensory intensity (0–5) across primary honey flavor and aroma dimensions: Floral, Fruity, Herbal & Fresh, Spicy & Warm, Sweet & Confectionary, Nutty & Woody, Earthy & Mineral, and Animal & Pungent.
_Avoid_: Honey spider graph, honey flavor wheel

**Honey Substitute**:
A varietal honey possessing sufficiently similar color, aroma intensity, and flavor profile to serve as an interchangeable alternative in meadmaking, brewing, or cider recipes.
_Avoid_: Alternative honey, replacement nectar

### Pomes & Varieties

**Pome Variety**:
A distinct cultivated variety of pome fruit (apple, *Malus domestica*, or perry pear, *Pyrus communis*) characterized by specific pomological, chemical, and sensory attributes for cider and perry production.
_Avoid_: Apple strain, cider fruit, pear type

**Pome Classification**:
The standardized four-tier classification system (Long Ashton / Barker model) categorizing cider apples and perry pears by titratable acidity (% malic acid) and polyphenol tannin content: Bittersweet, Bittersharp, Sharp, and Sweet.
_Avoid_: Apple category, fruit class, taste category

**Fruit Use**:
The culinary and cidermaking purpose of a pome variety: Heritage Cider/Perry Specimen (varieties cultivated specifically for fermentation tannins, acid, and phenolic complexity) or Culinary / Dessert (table and baking fruit contributing fermentable sugars, crisp acidity, and fresh aromatics).
_Avoid_: Apple purpose, fruit category, fruit role

**Bittersweet**:
A pome fruit classification characterized by low acidity (≤ 0.45% malic acid) and high tannin (≥ 0.20% polyphenols), providing body, astringency, and rich color without excessive tartness.
_Avoid_: Sweet bitter, soft tannin apple

**Bittersharp**:
A pome fruit classification characterized by high acidity (> 0.45% malic acid) and high tannin (≥ 0.20% polyphenols), providing both crisp sharpness and drying astringency for standalone or backbone blending.
_Avoid_: Sharp bitter, full-bodied tart apple

**Sharp**:
A pome fruit classification characterized by high acidity (> 0.45% malic acid) and low tannin (< 0.20% polyphenols), providing bright, crisp acidity and fresh fruit character.
_Avoid_: Acid apple, tart variety

**Sweet**:
A pome fruit classification characterized by low acidity (≤ 0.45% malic acid) and low tannin (< 0.20% polyphenols), providing fermentable sugars and fruit aromatics without notable astringency or sharpness.
_Avoid_: Dessert cider apple, mild fruit

**Long Ashton Matrix Gauge**:
A visual classification gauge mapping titratable acidity (malic acid %) against tannin concentration (polyphenol %) across the 0.45% acid and 0.20% tannin thresholds to plot a pome variety within the Long Ashton quadrant (Bittersweet, Bittersharp, Sharp, Sweet).
_Avoid_: Acid graph, tannin plot, apple gauge

**Sorbitol**:
A non-fermentable sugar alcohol naturally occurring in high concentrations in perry pears that contributes permanent residual sweetness, body, and unfermentable mouthfeel to finished perry.
_Avoid_: Pear sugar, fruit alcohol

**Pome Sensory Radar Chart**:
An 8-axis radial polygon chart plotting quantitative sensory intensity (0–5) across primary cider and perry flavor and aroma dimensions: Acidity / Sharpness, Tannin / Bitterness, Fresh Fruit, Baked / Ripe Fruit, Citrus & Tropical, Floral & Herbal, Earthy & Farmhouse, and Sweet & Confectionary.
_Avoid_: Cider spider graph, apple flavor wheel

**Pome Substitute**:
A pome fruit variety possessing sufficiently similar classification, tannin structure, and acid balance to serve as an interchangeable alternative in blending or single-varietal cidermaking.
_Avoid_: Replacement apple, alternative pome

### Yeast & Fermentation Cultures

**Yeast Strain**:
A distinct cultivated biological culture or isolate of fermentative yeast or brewing bacteria defined by specific physiological, attenuation, flocculation, and flavor profiles.
_Avoid_: Yeast variety, yeast type, bug, culture

**Microorganism Classification**:
The biological and brewing classification of the fermentation culture: Ale (*Saccharomyces cerevisiae*), Lager (*Saccharomyces pastorianus*), Wheat/Weizen, Kveik, Wild (*Brettanomyces*), Bacteria (*Lactobacillus* or *Pediococcus*), or Wine / Mead / Cider.
_Avoid_: Yeast class, bug species, culture type

**Attenuation**:
The percentage of wort or must sugars converted into alcohol and carbon dioxide during fermentation, categorized into minimum and maximum apparent attenuation ranges.
_Avoid_: Fermentation depth, sugar uptake

**Flocculation**:
The tendency of yeast cells to clump together into flocs and settle out of suspension toward the end of fermentation: Low, Medium, Medium-High, High, or Very High.
_Avoid_: Settling speed, sediment drop, dropping out

**Alcohol Tolerance**:
The maximum ethanol concentration (% ABV) a yeast strain can withstand before cell viability and fermentation activity cease.
_Avoid_: Kill level, ABV ceiling, alcohol limit

**Optimum Temperature Range**:
The recommended temperature band (°F / °C) for active fermentation that balances attenuation velocity with desirable ester and higher-alcohol production.
_Avoid_: Working temp, pitching band

**Diastaticus (STA1)**:
The presence of the *STA1* gene encoding extracellular glucoamylase, which hydrolyzes unfermentable dextrins and risks super-attenuation, bottle gushing, or cross-contamination in breweries.
_Avoid_: Wild gene, creeper strain

**Phenolic Off-Flavor (POF)**:
The genetic ability of a yeast strain (POF+) to enzymatically decarboxylate hydroxycinnamic acids into 4-vinyl guaiacol (clove-like phenols), required in German wheat and Belgian ales but considered a defect in clean lagers and American ales.
_Avoid_: Clove gene, spicy trait

**Commercial Yeast Equivalents**:
Representative commercial offerings across liquid and dry yeast laboratories (e.g., White Labs, Wyeast, Fermentis, Lallemand, Imperial, Omega) that isolate or package the canonical strain.
_Avoid_: Yeast brands, vendor codes

**Yeast Sensory Radar Chart**:
An 8-axis radial polygon chart plotting quantitative sensory intensity (0–5) across primary fermentation aroma and flavor dimensions: Clean/Neutral, Fruity Esters, Spicy Phenols, Tart/Acidity, Funky/Earthy, Sulfury/Crisp, Alcohol/Warmth, and Mouthfeel/Sweetness Retention.
_Avoid_: Yeast spider graph, fermentation flavor wheel

**Yeast Substitute**:
A yeast strain possessing sufficiently similar attenuation, flocculation, temperature tolerance, and sensory profile to serve as an interchangeable alternative in recipe design.
_Avoid_: Replacement strain, alternative yeast

### Mead & Alternative Guidelines

**Beverage Discipline**:
The high-level category of fermented craft beverage supported in the reference suite: Beer (BJCP 2021, Brewers Association 2026), Mead (BJCP Mead Guidelines), and Cider (BJCP Cider Guidelines).
_Avoid_: Beverage type, guide category

**Mead Style**:
A distinct recognized category of mead specified by honey varietal character, fruit/spice ingredients, sweetness level, carbonation, and strength in official BJCP Mead Guidelines (Categories M1–M4).
_Avoid_: Honey wine, mead recipe

**Mead Sweetness Classification**:
The sensory perception and target final gravity of residual sugar in a finished mead: Dry (1.000–1.010 FG), Semi-Sweet (1.010–1.025 FG), or Sweet (1.025–1.050+ FG).
_Avoid_: Sugar level, sweetness rating

**Mead Carbonation Classification**:
The carbonation and effervescence level of mead: Still (no bubbles), Pétillant (light effervescence), or Sparkling (lively, champagne-like carbonation).
_Avoid_: Bubble level, fizz level

**Mead Strength Classification**:
The classification of mead by alcohol strength: Hydromel (3.5–7.5% ABV), Standard (7.5–12.5% ABV), or Sack (12.5–14.0%+ ABV).
_Avoid_: Alcohol tier, mead ABV class

**Cider Style**:
A distinct recognized category of fermented apple or pear beverage specified by culinary/bittersweet apple varietals, fruit/spice adjuncts, sweetness tier, carbonation, and strength in official BJCP Cider Guidelines (Categories C1–C2).
_Avoid_: Hard cider recipe, apple drink

**Cider Sweetness Classification**:
The 5-tier classification of residual sweetness and final gravity in a finished cider: Dry (< 1.000 FG), Medium-Dry (1.000–1.005 FG), Medium (1.005–1.010 FG), Medium-Sweet (1.010–1.020 FG), or Sweet (> 1.020 FG).
_Avoid_: Sugar level, cider sweetness scale

**Cider Carbonation Classification**:
The carbonation and effervescence level of cider or perry: Still (no bubbles), Pétillant (light effervescence), or Sparkling (lively effervescence).
_Avoid_: Bubble level, fizz level

**Cider Strength Classification**:
The classification of cider by alcohol strength: Session (3.0–4.5% ABV), Standard (4.5–7.5% ABV), or High Gravity (7.5–13.0%+ ABV).
_Avoid_: Alcohol tier, cider gravity tier

**Discipline Icon**:
A contextual visual glyph in the navigation drawer and headers that automatically adapts to the currently active beverage discipline (a beer mug for Beer, a bee for Mead, an apple for Cider).
_Avoid_: Guide logo, beverage avatar

**Favorite**:
A user-bookmarked beverage style (across Beer, Mead, or Cider guidelines), canonical ingredient (Hop, Malt, Honey, Pome, or Yeast), or Off-Flavor saved for rapid cross-discipline reference.
_Avoid_: Bookmark, starred item, saved card, pinned item

### Off-Flavors & Sensory Evaluation

**Off-Flavor**:
A distinct chemical or sensory attribute detected in aroma, flavor, or mouthfeel (such as Diacetyl, Acetaldehyde, DMS, or Lightstruck) caused by fermentation stress, raw materials, brewing process errors, microbial contamination, or aging/oxidation—typically considered a judging fault, though permissible at controlled levels in specific styles.
_Avoid_: Defect, beer flaw, taint, blemish

**Off-Flavor Category**:
A standardized root-cause classification of an Off-Flavor based on the stage or mechanism where it arises: Fermentation & Yeast, Mashing & Boil, Sanitation & Microbial, Oxidation & Aging, or Water & Packaging.
_Avoid_: Fault type, flaw group, defect family

**Sensory Modality**:
The organoleptic channel through which an Off-Flavor is detected during evaluation: Aroma (olfactory/retronasal), Flavor (gustatory/palate), or Mouthfeel (trigeminal/tactile sensation such as astringency, slickness, or alcoholic warmth).
_Avoid_: Detection channel, taste mode, sense type

**Style Permissibility**:
Whether an Off-Flavor is unconditionally a fault across all fermented beverages (`Always a Fault`, such as Chlorophenol, Musty, or Lightstruck) or acceptable/expected at low-to-moderate concentrations in specific beverage styles (`Style-Permissible`, such as Diacetyl, Estery, Phenolic, Lactic, or Acetic).
_Avoid_: Fault severity, legality, style allowance

**Perception Threshold**:
The minimum concentration of a chemical compound in beer (typically expressed in ppm, ppb, or ppt) at which an average sensory panelist can detect its presence in aroma, flavor, or mouthfeel.
_Avoid_: Detection limit, taste floor, cutoff level

**Meilgaard Flavor Wheel**:
The standardized three-tiered sensory terminology system (developed by Morten Meilgaard for the ASBC, EBC, and MBAA) assigning numeric reference codes (e.g., 0620 for Diacetyl, 0150 for Acetaldehyde) to distinct aroma, flavor, and mouthfeel attributes.
_Avoid_: Aroma wheel code, ASBC number, flavor index
