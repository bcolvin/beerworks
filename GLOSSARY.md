# Beerworks

Domain terminology for beer style reference guidelines, hop varieties, and sensory evaluation in the Beerworks suite.

## Language

### Hops & Ingredients

**Hop Variety**:
A distinct cultivated variety of the hop plant (*Humulus lupulus*) defined by specific agronomic, chemical, and sensory characteristics.
_Avoid_: Hop strain, hop type

**Lineage**:
The botanical parentage and breeding progeny of a hop variety, identifying parent cultivars and descendant varieties.
_Avoid_: Pedigree, family tree

**Purpose**:
The primary brewing application of a hop variety, classified as Bittering, Aroma, or Dual-Purpose based on alpha acid concentration and essential oil composition.
_Avoid_: Hop role, usage category

**Sensory Category**:
A standardized high-level organoleptic grouping (such as Citrus, Tropical Fruit, Floral, Herbal, Spicy, Pine/Resinous, Earthy/Woody) used to classify hop aroma profiles.
_Avoid_: Flavor group, aroma family

**Sensory Descriptor**:
A specific descriptive term (e.g., grapefruit, passionfruit, cedar, black pepper) identifying a nuanced aroma or flavor note within a sensory category.
_Avoid_: Tasting note, flavor tag

**Sensory Radar Chart**:
A multi-axis radial polygon chart plotting quantitative sensory intensity (0–5) across primary hop aroma dimensions (Citrus, Tropical, Floral, Herbal, Spicy, Pine, Earthy, Grassy).
_Avoid_: Spider graph, aroma wheel

**Alpha Acid**:
The primary resinous compound class (humulone group) in hops that isomerizes during boiling to impart bitterness to beer.
_Avoid_: Bittering rating, AA% (in formal descriptions)

**Beta Acid**:
The secondary resinous compound class (lupulone group) in hops that contributes subtle bitterness through oxidative aging rather than thermal isomerization.
_Avoid_: Preservative rating, BA% (in formal descriptions)

**Hop Substitute**:
A hop cultivar possessing sufficiently similar alpha acid potential, essential oil ratios, and sensory descriptors to serve as an interchangeable alternative in recipe formulation.
_Avoid_: Replacement hop, alternative variety

**Common Hops**:
The primary hop varieties traditionally, historically, or commercially associated with a specific beer style in brewing literature and competition guidelines.
_Avoid_: Featured hops, style hops, associated hops

### Malts & Fermentables

**Malt Variety**:
A distinct type of malted or unmalted brewing grain or fermentable characterized by specific malting/kilning processes, color rating, diastatic power, and organoleptic properties.
_Avoid_: Malt type, grain variety

**Malt Category**:
A standardized classification of brewing grains based on production method and recipe function: Base Malt, Kilned & Toasted, Caramel & Crystal, Roasted & Dark, or Adjunct & Raw Grain.
_Avoid_: Grain class, malt family

**Diastatic Power**:
The enzymatic capacity of malted grain to convert starches into fermentable sugars during mashing, measured in degrees Lintner or qualitative conversion levels.
_Avoid_: Enzyme level, conversion strength

**Extract Potential**:
The laboratory yield of soluble extract obtained from grain under standardized fine-grind conditions (Dry Basis Fine Grind %), indicating gravity contribution per pound.
_Avoid_: Sugar yield, PPG potential

**Typical Usage**:
The primary brewing application, recipe role, and maximum grist percentage recommended for a malt (e.g., base grist, foam stability, color adjustment, roasted notes).
_Avoid_: Usage notes, recipe tips

**Functional Usage Tag**:
A standardized classification of a malt's specific physical or recipe contribution in brewing (e.g., Base Grist, Head Retention, Body & Mouthfeel, Color Adjustment, Caramel Sweetness, Toasty Aroma, Roast & Coffee, Haze & Cloudiness).
_Avoid_: Brewing role, grain function

**Malt Sensory Radar Chart**:
An 8-axis radial polygon chart plotting quantitative sensory intensity (0–5) across primary malt flavor dimensions (Malty/Sweet, Bready/Biscuit, Toasty, Caramel/Toffee, Nutty, Chocolate, Roast/Coffee, Grainy/Husky).
_Avoid_: Malt spider graph, grain wheel

**Malt Substitute**:
A malt product or combination of grains providing equivalent color, extract, and flavor characteristics that can be swapped into a grist formulation without altering style character.
_Avoid_: Alternative malt, replacement grain

**Commercial Malts**:
Representative commercial brand offerings and notable maltster products (e.g., Weyermann Carafa, Briess Carapils, Dingemans Special B) exemplifying a canonical malt variety.
_Avoid_: Malt brands, proprietary grains

### Beer Styles & Evaluation

**Beer Style**:
A distinct recognized category of beer specified by sensory attributes, cultural history, and technical brewing parameters in official guidelines (BJCP or Brewers Association).
_Avoid_: Beer type, style profile

**Base Style Group**:
A top-level grouping of related beer styles sharing common lineage or geographical origin (e.g., IPA, Standard American Beer, Pale Malty European Lager).
_Avoid_: Supercategory, broad style

**Side-by-Side Comparison**:
A concurrent comparative evaluation of two or three beer styles, hop varieties, or malt profiles aligning technical parameters, sensory radars, and flavor descriptors in a multi-column matrix.
_Avoid_: Diff view, duel mode

### Mead & Alternative Guidelines

**Beverage Discipline**:
The high-level category of fermented craft beverage supported in the reference suite: Beer (BJCP 2021, Brewers Association 2026), Mead (BJCP Mead Guidelines), and Cider (BJCP Cider Guidelines).
_Avoid_: Beverage type, guide category

**Mead Style**:
A distinct recognized category of mead specified by honey varietal character, fruit/spice ingredients, sweetness level, carbonation, and strength in official BJCP Mead Guidelines (Categories M1–M4).
_Avoid_: Honey wine, mead recipe

**Mead Sweetness Classification**:
The sensory perception and target final gravity of residual sugar in a finished mead: Dry (1.000–1.010 FG), Semi-Sweet (1.010–1.025 FG), or Sweet (1.025–1.050+ FG).
_Avoid_: Sugar level, sweetness rating

**Mead Carbonation Classification**:
The carbonation and effervescence level of mead: Still (no bubbles), Pétillant (light effervescence), or Sparkling (lively, champagne-like carbonation).
_Avoid_: Bubble level, fizz level

**Mead Strength Classification**:
The classification of mead by alcohol strength: Hydromel (3.5–7.5% ABV), Standard (7.5–12.5% ABV), or Sack (12.5–14.0%+ ABV).
_Avoid_: Alcohol tier, mead ABV class

**Discipline Icon**:
A contextual visual glyph in the navigation drawer and headers that automatically adapts to the currently active beverage discipline (a beer mug for Beer, a bee for Mead, an apple for Cider).
_Avoid_: Guide logo, beverage avatar
